# Clairity Product Constitution

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Approved  
**Owner:** Claire  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-23  
**Authority:** Highest-level governing document for Clairity

---

## Purpose

This Constitution defines the principles that all Clairity product, design, architecture and implementation work must follow. A proposed change that conflicts with these principles must be raised explicitly and approved before implementation.

## Constitutional principles

### C-01 — Optimise for Claire

Clairity is a private, single-user product. It must optimise for Claire’s real routines, needs, commitment and evidence rather than generic onboarding, social engagement, growth or use by other people.

### C-02 — Evidence before conclusions

Clairity exists to collect evidence, preserve context and support better-informed decisions. It must distinguish recorded fact, calculation, observation, association, hypothesis and unsupported assumption.

### C-03 — Reduce friction without reducing evidence

Clairity may adapt presentation, pacing and interaction effort, but it must not systematically collect less core evidence during low-energy, low-motivation or difficult periods.

### C-04 — Specific questions, independent interactions

Logging must use specific evidence prompts rather than vague umbrella questions. Evidence areas must be independently loggable without a required start, submit, save or finish flow.

### C-05 — Autosave every valid input

Every valid interaction must be preserved immediately. Leaving the app must not discard completed or partially completed evidence.

### C-06 — Missing data has meaning

Unrecorded, unknown, none, zero, skipped and not applicable are different states. Clairity must not invent an answer from non-interaction.

### C-07 — Preserve history

Retrospective entries and corrections are valid evidence. Disabling or retiring a module must preserve its historical data unless Claire explicitly requests deletion.

### C-08 — Modular by default

Evidence areas and life-stage capabilities must use shared module contracts and services. New modules should be addable and obsolete modules removable from active use without significant rework.

### C-09 — Privacy by default

Personal evidence, secrets and credentials must never be committed to a public repository. Data collection, analytics, export, storage and integrations must be proportionate and transparent.

### C-10 — Be explicit about uncertainty

Clairity must not present correlation as causation or act as a diagnostic product. Insights must communicate evidence strength, limitations and plausible alternative explanations.

### C-11 — Every data point needs a purpose

Clairity should not collect information merely because it may be interesting. Evidence must support a defined question, pattern, decision or future capability.

### C-12 — No patch architecture

Implementation convenience must not override agreed product decisions. Local fixes must not introduce duplicate state, parallel flows, hidden exceptions or conflicting terminology.

### C-13 — Feasibility is part of the decision

Meaningful proposals must state whether they are straightforward, feasible with trade-offs, risky, expensive or better deferred.

### C-14 — Disengagement is evidence, not failure

A period of non-use may reveal friction, sleep disruption, low motivation or unsuitable routines. Any later review or re-engagement must be curious and non-judgemental.

## Authority order

Where documents appear to conflict, use this order:

1. Product Constitution
2. Approved Product Requirements
3. Later Decision Log entry
4. Technical Architecture
5. Design System
6. Backlog
7. Implementation convenience

A conflict must be resolved in documentation before code establishes new behaviour.

---

## Amendment rule

The Constitution may only change through an explicit product decision approved by Claire. A constitutional change requires:

- a new Decision Log entry;
- a major documentation version;
- updates to affected requirements and architecture;
- a clear statement of what is superseded.
